Smart Table vs Toast: Switching Your POS vs Adding a Guest Layer

These two names get compared as if they were rivals for the same job. They are not: Toast is a full point-of-sale platform you switch to; Smart Table is a guest layer you add to the Square POS you keep. The real question is which decision your restaurant actually faces.

By the Smart Table team · November 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Full disclosure: we build Smart Table, so weigh our framing accordingly — but the framing matters. If your point of sale itself is failing you, that is a POS decision, and Toast is a serious candidate for it. If your POS is fine and what fails is the last ten minutes of the meal — the wait for the bill, the split-cheque shuffle — that is a guest-layer decision, and it requires replacing nothing. Confusing the two is how restaurants sign a POS contract to solve a payment problem.

What Toast is — and where it genuinely wins

Toast is a point-of-sale platform built specifically for restaurants, and that focus shows. Toast's whole stack — terminals, handheld devices, kitchen displays, online ordering and a deep bench of restaurant-specific features — was designed around how restaurants actually run. For a restaurant that has outgrown its setup and wants one vendor accountable for the whole stack, that is a real and legitimate appeal.

Choosing Toast means adopting its ecosystem: its hardware, its workflows, its contract. Its pricing varies by plan — check their site for current rates. The strengths are depth and cohesion; the commitment is total. That is the right trade for some restaurants and an oversized one for others.

What switching your POS actually involves

A POS switch is the biggest technology project a small restaurant takes on. Hardware arrives and gets installed; the menu is rebuilt item by item; staff are retrained on new flows while still serving guests; contract, processing and reporting all change hands; and there is a cutover — a night after which the new one simply has to work, mid-service, on a Friday. Done well, it can be worth it. But it is measured in weeks of attention, not hours.

What Smart Table is instead

Smart Table is not a point of sale at all. It is a guest layer that runs on top of Square: it connects to your Square account, reads your menu and open cheques, and lets guests order and pay from their own phone: scan a QR code, split the bill between themselves, tip, and pay by card in the browser — no app download. Payments are recorded back in Square, your staff watch every table from a live dashboard, and your terminals stay in service for guests who prefer them. Adding it takes an afternoon, not a project: connect the account, print the codes, enable ordering per location if you want it.

And the honest constraint, stated plainly: Smart Table works with Square today. If your restaurant runs Toast and you are keeping Toast, Smart Table is not an option for you today — we would rather say that up front than in a footnote.

Which decision are you actually making?

Evaluate Toast (and its POS rivals) if your POS is the problem: you have outgrown it, restaurant-specific depth is missing, reporting or kitchen flow holds you back, or you are opening a room and choosing its foundation. Take it slowly; it will shape years of service.

Add Smart Table if your POS is fine and the end of the meal is not: tables occupied by finished guests waiting to pay, servers spending their peak hour running cards, split bills stalling sections. Square keeps doing its job; the guest layer removes the bottleneck Square was never designed to remove — the queue of guests waiting for a person and a device.

Note what is not on either list: a platform migration bought to solve a payment problem. The wait at the end of the meal is expensive — we priced it in how much slow payment costs your restaurant — but it is a narrow problem, and narrow problems deserve narrow, reversible fixes.

Frequently asked questions

Does Smart Table work with Toast?

No. Today Smart Table works with Square — it reads your menu and open cheques from Square and writes guest orders and payments back. If Toast is your point of sale and you plan to keep it, Smart Table cannot be added today. It becomes an option only if your restaurant runs on Square.

Is Smart Table a cheaper alternative to Toast?

They are different categories, so prices are not directly comparable. Toast is a full point-of-sale platform whose pricing varies by plan — check their site. Smart Table is an add-on layer priced in CAD per location: Starter is $49.99 per month or 1% per card payment; Base is $99 per month or 2%. Compare totals only if you are pricing a whole new POS stack against keeping Square and adding a guest layer.

What is involved in switching POS versus adding Smart Table?

Switching your point of sale is a project: new hardware, your menu rebuilt, staff retrained, contract terms reviewed, and a cutover night where everything must work. Adding Smart Table is an afternoon: connect Square, print a QR code per table, enable ordering per location if you want it, and brief the team. Guests who prefer the old way keep it — your terminals stay where they are.

Can I try Smart Table before making any bigger decision?

Yes — that is what the Founding Pilot is for. Starter runs free for 30 days with no card required, on your existing Square account, so you can judge the guest layer across a month of real services first. If your restaurant does not run Square today, start with the live demo.
