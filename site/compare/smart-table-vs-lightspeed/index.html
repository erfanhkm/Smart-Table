Smart Table vs Lightspeed: A Full POS vs a Guest Layer on Square

Lightspeed is a complete point-of-sale platform — the kind you choose when the POS itself is the decision. Smart Table is a guest ordering and payment layer that works with Square today. This page is honest about both, starting with the fact that they do different jobs.

By the Smart Table team · November 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Full disclosure: we build Smart Table. And the first honest thing to say on a page with this title is that Smart Table does not connect to Lightspeed. Smart Table works with Square today — it reads menus and open cheques from Square and writes guest orders and payments back. So this is not a feature shootout. It is a guide to two different decisions: replacing your point of sale, or adding a guest layer to the one you have.

What Lightspeed is — and where it genuinely wins

Lightspeed is a full point-of-sale platform from a Canadian company — founded in Montreal — with a long track record across restaurants and hospitality. Its strengths are the strengths of a mature, complete system: deep menu and floor management, particularly strong inventory tools, serious multi-location management, and the reporting depth that comes with that scale. Restaurants choose it when the POS is load-bearing infrastructure and they want one established platform accountable for it.

Lightspeed's pricing varies by plan — check their site for current rates. What matters here is the shape of the commitment: adopting a full POS means its hardware, its workflows and its contract, with a migration to get there. For a restaurant whose POS genuinely holds it back — or a group standardizing several locations — that commitment is often exactly right.

What Smart Table is instead

Smart Table is not a point of sale, and does not try to be one. It is the guest-facing layer for the end of the meal, built to run on Square: a QR code on each table opens your menu and that table's live bill on the guest's phone; guests can order, split the cheque — equally, by item, or a custom amount — tip, and pay by card in the browser, with no app download and no account. Payments record back into Square, and your staff watch every table's state from a live dashboard instead of discovering at the till who is still waiting to settle.

It also closes the loop after payment: every guest is offered both a public Google review and private feedback, with no star pre-screen, and a low rating alerts your team instantly, while the guest is still seated. None of this replaces anything: your Square terminals keep serving guests who prefer a person and a card machine, and cash works exactly as today.

Which decision are you actually making?

Evaluate Lightspeed (and its POS rivals) if the POS itself is your problem: you need inventory or multi-location depth your current system cannot give you, reporting is a monthly wrestling match, or you are fitting out a new room and choosing its foundation. Take that decision slowly — demos, references, a hard look at contract terms — because you will live with it for years.

Add Smart Table if your Square POS works and the end of the meal does not: finished tables waiting on the bill, split cheques stalling a section, your best servers spending the peak hour as card couriers. That bottleneck survives every POS migration, because no POS removes it — it lives between the guest and the device, not inside the software. A guest layer removes it directly, and reversibly: if it does not earn its place in a month of Fridays, the QR codes come off the tables and nothing else has changed.

The mistake to avoid is the blended one: migrating your entire point of sale to fix a ten-minute problem at the end of the meal — or expecting a ten-minute fix to substitute for a POS you have genuinely outgrown. Name the problem first; the product category follows.

Frequently asked questions

Does Smart Table integrate with Lightspeed?

No. Smart Table works with Square today — it reads your menu and open cheques from Square and writes guest orders and payments back. It does not connect to Lightspeed, so if Lightspeed is your point of sale and you plan to keep it, Smart Table is not something you can add today. We would rather state that plainly than imply a connection that does not exist.

Is Lightspeed a good point of sale?

For many restaurants, yes. Lightspeed is a mature, full-featured POS from a Canadian company, with particular depth in inventory and multi-location management and a long track record across hospitality. If your point of sale itself is the thing holding your operation back, Lightspeed belongs on the shortlist of platforms to evaluate properly, alongside its POS rivals.

What does Smart Table cost compared with Lightspeed?

They are different categories, so treat any total comparison with care. Lightspeed is a full point-of-sale platform whose pricing varies by plan — check their site for current rates. Smart Table is an add-on layer priced in CAD per location: Starter is $49.99 per month or 1% per card payment, and Base is $99 per month or 2%, on top of the Square account it runs with. The Founding Pilot runs Starter free for 30 days with no card required.

Who should choose which?

Choose a new POS — Lightspeed or a rival — when the point of sale itself is your problem: missing depth, weak multi-location tools, or a room being opened from scratch. Add Smart Table when your Square POS works fine and the problem is narrower: guests waiting to pay, split bills stalling sections, servers spending peak hours running cards. Different problems deserve different sizes of fix.
