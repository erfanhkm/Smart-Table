The formulas, in plain sight

  • Contribution margin = 100% − variable cost %. It is the share of each sales dollar available to cover fixed costs.
  • Break-even sales = monthly fixed costs ÷ contribution margin. Break-even guests = break-even sales ÷ average spend per guest.
  • Guests per day assumes a 30-day month. If you are open fewer days — 26, say — divide the monthly guests by your open days instead; your break-even per open day will be higher.
  • Variable cost % should hold everything that scales with each guest: food and beverage cost, hourly labour, card processing. Salaried staff belong in fixed costs, not in the percentage — mixing the two double-counts them.
  • Average spend per guest is held constant, and sales figures read cleanest net of sales tax. The pre-filled numbers are an illustrative example, not a typical restaurant. Nothing you type is sent anywhere or stored — the arithmetic runs in your browser.

A monthly line you can actually manage to

"We need a good month" is not a target. Break-even turns it into two numbers a team can hold in their heads: the sales the month must reach, and the guests each day must bring. It also shows you which lever moves the line. A rent increase raises it dollar for dollar; a two-point rise in food cost raises it too, quietly, through the contribution margin; a higher average spend lowers the guest count without lowering the sales line at all. Owners re-run this arithmetic whenever a lease, a wage change or a menu reprint moves a cost — and before signing anything with a second location's name on it. How the payment end of the floor feeds the same monthly math — in labour hours, not percentages — is the subject of QR ordering vs handheld POS.

Above the line is where the decisions live

Break-even is a floor, not a goal: everything above it is profit, everything below it is a loss the month has to be carried through. Knowing the floor tells you how much room a slow February actually has, what a new hire does to the line, and whether a discount promotion can possibly pay for itself in extra covers. Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my restaurant's break-even point?

Break-even sales = monthly fixed costs ÷ contribution margin, where contribution margin = 100% minus your variable cost percentage. Break-even guests = break-even sales ÷ your average spend per guest. This calculator does that arithmetic and spreads the monthly guests over a 30-day month to give you a per-day figure.

What counts as a fixed cost, and what counts as variable?

Fixed costs arrive whether you serve ten guests or a thousand: rent, salaried staff, insurance, licences, base utilities, subscriptions. Variable costs scale with each guest: food and beverage cost, hourly labour, card processing fees. If a cost is partly both — a salaried chef plus hourly line cooks — put the salaried part in fixed costs and the hourly part in the variable percentage.

Why does the calculator spread guests over a 30-day month?

A monthly guest target is hard to act on; a daily one is not. If you are open fewer than 30 days — 26, say — divide the monthly break-even guests by your open days instead: your break-even per open day will be higher than the per-calendar-day figure shown.

Is break-even the same as making a profit?

No. Break-even is the sales level where the costs you entered are exactly covered — profit is everything above it, and a loss is everything below. It also only covers what you enter: if you want your own salary, a loan payment or a repair fund covered too, add them to fixed costs.
