The assumptions, in plain sight

  • Payments per month = tables × turns per night × nights open per month. Every seating ends in one payment.
  • Server time per payment ≈ 4 minutes. Printing and fetching the bill, delivering it and returning, fetching the terminal and processing, then receipts or change. Your room may be faster or slower — the shape of the math is the same.
  • Extra tables assumes each table frees up your full wait time earlier on every turn, measured against a 90-minute average turn. It is a potential: it only becomes real covers on nights when demand is waiting and the kitchen can keep up.
  • Guest-wait time counts one wait per table, not per guest — a table of four waiting ten minutes is ten table-minutes here, not forty guest-minutes. If anything, this understates it.
  • Nothing on this page is a statistic about your restaurant. It is your own inputs, multiplied honestly.

Why the wait costs more than it looks

Paying the traditional way is a relay: flag the server, print and deliver the bill, wait for the server to return, fetch the terminal, process and split the cards, then receipts and change. No step is outrageous — the cost is the sum of the steps plus the queue at each one, and the queue peaks exactly when the room is fullest, because every table runs the same relay at the same time. That is why the wait quietly eats your last Friday-night turn and your servers' attention at the moment new tables are sitting down. What removes it

You cannot fix a queue by running it faster; you remove the trips. When guests settle on their own phone — scanning the table's QR code, reviewing the itemized bill, splitting it equally, by item or with group pay, tipping, choosing a receipt — emailed, or printed where a device is fitted, and leaving a rating — payment happens in parallel at every table instead of one terminal at a time, and your servers spend those recovered hours on guests. Smart Table does this alongside your existing Square account, with no app for guests to download; try the guest side in the live demo or read the complete guide to QR code payment. Guests who prefer the terminal keep it — the wait becomes a choice, not a queue.

Frequently asked questions

How does the Payment Wait Calculator work?

It multiplies your inputs together. Payments per month = tables × turns per night × nights open. Guest-wait hours = payments × your average wait in minutes ÷ 60. Server hours use a stated assumption of about 4 server-minutes of trips per payment (printing and delivering the bill, fetching the terminal, processing, receipts). Labour dollars = server hours × your server wage. Extra tables per night assumes each table frees up your wait minutes earlier on every turn, measured against a 90-minute average turn.

Are these numbers real statistics about my restaurant?

No — and that is deliberate. There is no honest universal statistic for how long guests wait to pay; it depends entirely on your room and your night. This calculator does transparent arithmetic on the numbers you enter, with its two assumptions (about 4 server-minutes per payment; a 90-minute average turn) stated on the page. Measure your own wait for one weekend — plates cleared to cheque closed in your POS — and enter that.

Does the labour figure mean I can cut those hours from my schedule?

Not automatically. Your servers are on shift either way, so treat the labour figure as capacity, not cash savings: it is the paid time currently spent on payment trips that could instead go to guests, extra tables, or a calmer floor. Whether any of it becomes a scheduling saving is your decision, and any vendor who promises it as guaranteed cash is overselling.

What would remove the wait in my restaurant?

Removing the trips. When guests settle the cheque on their own phone — scanning the table's QR code, reviewing the itemized bill, splitting it, tipping, choosing a receipt and paying — the server stops shuttling between the table, the printer and the terminal, and tables settle in parallel instead of one device at a time. Smart Table does this alongside your existing Square account, and the traditional terminal route stays available for any guest who prefers it.
